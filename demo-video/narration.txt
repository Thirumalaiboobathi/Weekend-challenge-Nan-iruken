[HOOK]
If your mother falls at home, who finds out — and when?

[PROBLEM]
Millions of parents in India live alone while their children work in other cities. A daily phone call gets missed, and nothing escalates. Naan Irukken — 'I'm here' — fixes that with one tap a day.

[DEMO1]
Every morning she gets one button. If she doesn't answer, it gently reminds her, then alerts family one by one. When someone says they're checking, everyone else is told — and the system keeps waiting until they confirm she's safe. If they don't, it re-escalates.

[DEMO2]
If she needs help, one tap alerts everyone at once. If no one responds, she's told to call 108.

[DEMO3]
She doesn't even need the button — any message counts as 'I'm here'.

[ARCH]
Each day's check-in is one AWS Step Functions Standard execution, started by EventBridge Scheduler. Every wait is a task token with a timeout, so nothing runs — and nothing costs — while it waits. Button taps come through API Gateway to Lambda, which resumes the workflow. DynamoDB holds the state, and S3 keeps a private audit trail.

[DASH]
Families get a private dashboard: the month at a glance, routine changes, and the real cost — a fraction of a rupee per family.

[CLOSE]
One SAM template, one deploy command, and an escalation pattern anyone can reuse for lone workers, medication reminders, or approvals. Naan Irukken.
